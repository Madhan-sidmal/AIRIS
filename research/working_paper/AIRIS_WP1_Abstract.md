**Title:**
Digital Infrastructure and Labour Market Adjustment During AI Diffusion: Evidence from Rural India

**JEL Classification:** O15, J21, J24, O33, R11

**Keywords:** artificial intelligence, labour market adjustment, digital infrastructure, BharatNet, rural India, difference-in-differences, structural transformation

---

## Abstract

We study whether pre-existing digital connectivity shapes the speed and character of labour market adjustment during periods of rapid AI diffusion. Using a difference-in-differences design with district and year fixed effects, we exploit variation in the timing of BharatNet optical fibre rollout across 27 rural districts in Karnataka and Rajasthan, India, and compare unemployment trajectories between early- and late-connected districts from 2019-20 to 2023-24 — a period that spans the global diffusion of large language model technologies. Early-connected districts experienced 3.03 percentage points higher unemployment growth relative to late-connected districts (95% CI: [0.07, 5.84]; wild cluster bootstrap p=0.040, G=27). Heterogeneity analysis reveals that this aggregate result reflects two qualitatively distinct adjustment processes operating simultaneously in different district types. In districts with above-median literacy rates, unemployment increased by 4.41 percentage points (p<0.001) while services sector employment simultaneously expanded by 3.42 percentage points (p<0.001), consistent with active structural transformation — workers transitioning toward service-sector employment with frictional unemployment during the rotation. In districts with below-median literacy rates, agricultural employment declined by 3.96 percentage points (p=0.046) without corresponding unemployment increases, suggesting agricultural exit toward unobserved destinations. The unemployment effect is entirely concentrated in high-agriculture districts (+4.90pp, p=0.003) and absent in low-agriculture districts (−0.26pp, p=0.935). These findings suggest that connectivity is a necessary but insufficient condition for smooth labour market adjustment during AI diffusion: human capital determines whether enhanced connectivity enables structural transformation or primarily exposes workers to demand-side disruption. Results are stable across five sensitivity specifications and use of wild cluster bootstrap inference.

## Short Abstract (50 words, for conference submissions)

We show that BharatNet digital connectivity amplifies labour market disruption during AI diffusion in rural India, but the effect is heterogeneous: high-literacy connected districts experience both rising unemployment and expanding services employment — consistent with structural transformation — while high-agriculture districts experience disruption without visible reallocation. Human capital moderates connectivity's role.

## Non-Technical Summary

India's BharatNet programme has extended optical fibre connectivity to rural areas, with some districts connected years before others. This timing variation allows us to compare what happened to rural labour markets in better-connected versus less-connected areas during the period (2019–2024) when AI tools became widely available.

We find that better-connected districts saw unemployment rise faster — by about 3 percentage points more than less-connected districts. But this is not a simple bad-news story. When we look at which districts drove this result, we find that in districts where people are more educated, both unemployment *and* service-sector jobs grew simultaneously. Workers were moving into new types of employment, but the transition took time — creating temporary unemployment. In less-educated, more agricultural districts, the connectivity effect was primarily disruptive: unemployment spiked without new jobs appearing to replace what was lost.

The policy implication is clear: connecting rural areas to the internet is important, but connectivity alone is not enough. Skills investment must accompany infrastructure investment to ensure that rural workers can benefit from — rather than merely be exposed to — the disruptions of the AI era.
